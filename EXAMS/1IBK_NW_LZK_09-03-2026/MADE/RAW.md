### States of Matter and Properties of Matter

#### States of Matter
[DEFINITION] The states of matter (solid, liquid, gaseous, and plasma) influence the form and volume of substances.
*   **Solid (Fest):** A solid body is either crystalline or amorphous. In a crystalline body, atoms or molecules are regularly arranged (e.g., most minerals and metals). In an amorphous state, atoms or molecules are irregularly arranged (e.g., glass). Solids have a constant shape and constant volume.
*   **Liquid (Flüssig):** In a liquid, particles are still close together but in stronger motion than in solids. Weak binding forces exist between the particles. Water is the most important liquid for most living beings. Liquids have a variable shape but a constant volume.
*   **Gaseous (Gasförmig):** In a gas, particles move far apart from each other and evenly fill the available space. Compared to a solid, a gas of the same mass occupies 1000 to 2000 times more space. Gases have a variable shape and variable volume.
*   **Plasma (Plasmaförmig):** A physical plasma consists of charged particles or a mixture of neutral and charged particles (atoms, molecules). Plasmas occur in fluorescent tubes and plasma screens. More than 98% of the visible matter in the universe is in the plasma state. Plasmas have a variable shape and variable volume.

[INFO] The state of matter a substance is in depends on temperature. By adding or removing heat, a specific state of matter can be achieved.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Diagram showing particle arrangement and movement in solid, liquid, gas, and plasma states, along with their characteristics regarding form and volume.]

#### Transitions between States of Matter
The state of matter of a substance changes depending on temperature.
*   **Melting (Schmelzen):** Solid to Liquid
*   **Freezing/Solidification (Erstarren):** Liquid to Solid
*   **Evaporation (Verdampfen):** Liquid to Gaseous
*   **Condensation (Kondensieren):** Gaseous to Liquid
*   **Sublimation (Sublimieren):** Solid to Gaseous
*   **Resublimation (Resublimieren):** Gaseous to Solid

[INFO] For example, water is solid below 0 °C and gaseous above 100 °C. The states of matter (solid, liquid, gaseous) thus depend on temperature and the input of heat. This applies not only to water but to all other substances. Some elements require very low temperatures to become liquid or solid, while others only melt and boil at very high temperatures.

**Examples of Melting and Boiling Points:**

#### Density
[DEFINITION] Density is a material property that relates the mass of a body to its volume. The ratio of the mass `m` of a body to its volume `V` is called density `ρ` (Greek letter Rho).

**Formula:**
`ρ = m / V`

**SI Unit of Density:** `kg/m³` (kilograms per cubic meter)

[INFO] The formula means: The density of a body is greater the greater its mass for the same volume, or the smaller its volume for the same mass.

**Examples of Material Densities (at 20°C and sea level):**
*   1 cm³ of air has a mass of 0.0012 g → Density of air is 0.0012 g/cm³ or 1.2 kg/m³.
*   1 cm³ of spruce wood has a mass of about 0.5 g → Density of spruce wood is 0.5 g/cm³ or 500 kg/m³.
*   1 cm³ of water has a mass of 1.00 g → Density of water is 1 g/cm³ or 1000 kg/m³.
*   1 cm³ of platinum has a mass of 21.45 g → Density of platinum is 21.45 g/cm³ or 21,450 kg/m³.

[INFO] Most substances become denser as temperature decreases because the particles move closer together.

#### Conductivity
[DEFINITION] In physics, a conductor is a substance that can transport energy or particles. How well the substance conducts is described by its "conductivity." There are conductors for electricity, heat, light, and magnetism. Below, we focus on electrical conduction.

[DEFINITION] The electrical conductance `G` indicates how well a conductor conducts electric current. Its unit is Siemens: `[G] = S`.

**Testing Conductivity:**
Whether substances conduct electricity or not can be determined simply. An electrical circuit (more information on circuits can be found on page 119 ff.) is set up with a battery and a small lamp, and interrupted at one point. The substance whose conductivity is to be tested is inserted at this point. Good conductors make the lamp light up brightly, moderate conductors make it light up dimly, and non-conductors do not light it up at all.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Simple circuit diagram for testing conductivity with a battery, light bulb, and a "test object" slot.]

**How does electrical conduction work?**
A substance conducts electricity well if it has freely movable charge carriers (electrons, ions). How current flows depends on whether it is a metal, a semiconductor, a non-conductor (insulator), a conductive liquid (electrolyte), or a gas.

*   **Conductors (mainly metals):** Negatively charged electrons move largely freely. Metals are therefore good electrical conductors; silver is the best. Copper and aluminum have slightly lower conductivity but are lighter and cheaper.
    [DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Diagram showing electrons moving freely within a metal lattice.]
*   **Non-conductors (Insulators):** These conduct electricity very poorly or not at all. They can therefore be used to shield a conductive material from other parts, which is why they are also called insulators. Good non-conductors include rubber, glass, porcelain, diamond, distilled water, and air.
*   **Semiconductors:** Their conductivity lies between that of metals and non-conductors. Semiconductors like silicon and germanium are solids with special properties: At temperatures near absolute zero, they have no free charge carriers and are therefore non-conductors. As the temperature rises, they conduct current better because heat releases charge carriers. An important application is in electronic components like diodes and transistors.

#### Viscosity

#### Aggregatzustände
[DEFINITION] Die Aggregatzustände (fest, flüssig, gasförmig und plasmaförmig) beeinflussen Form und Volumen der Stoffe.
*   **Fest:** Ein Festkörper ist entweder kristallin oder amorph. In einem kristallinen Körper sind Atome oder Moleküle regelmäßig angeordnet (z. B. die meisten Mineralien und Metalle). Im amorphen Zustand sind Atome oder Moleküle unregelmäßig angeordnet (z. B. in Glas). Festkörper haben eine konstante Form und ein konstantes Volumen.

Wasser ist die für die meisten Lebewesen wichtigste Flüssigkeit. Flüssigkeiten haben eine variable Form, aber ein konstantes Volumen.
*   **Gasförmig:** In einem Gas bewegen sich die Teilchen in großem Abstand voneinander und füllen den verfügbaren Raum gleichmäßig aus. Im Vergleich zu einem Festkörper nimmt ein Gas gleicher Masse den 1000- bis 2000-fachen Raum ein. Gase haben eine variable Form und ein variables Volumen.

Plasmen kommen in Leuchtstoffröhren und Plasmabildschirmen vor. Mehr als 98 % der sichtbaren Materie im Universum befindet sich im Plasmazustand. Plasmen haben eine variable Form und ein variables Volumen.

*   **Leiter (hauptsächlich Metalle):** Negativ geladene Elektronen bewegen sich weitgehend frei. Die Metalle sind daher gute Stromleiter, der beste davon ist Silber. Die Leitfähigkeit von Kupfer und Aluminium ist etwas geringer, dafür sind sie leichter und billiger.

### Energy

#### What is Energy?
[DEFINITION] Energy is the ability to do work.
[INFO] The physicist James Prescott Joule (1818-1889) investigated the equivalence of mechanical work and heat.

**Unit of Energy:** Joule (J)
`1 J = 1 Nm = 1 Ws`
[INFO] 1 Joule is a fairly small unit. To raise 1 kg of water by 1°C, an amount of heat of 4190 Joule is required.

**Why do we need energy?**
Energy is essential for various physical processes and daily life activities, including:
*   Households (heating, lighting, appliances)
*   Industry (production, machinery)
*   Transport (vehicles, trains, planes)

#### Forms of Energy
The following overview shows various forms of energy:

*   **Mechanical Energy:**
    *   **Potential Energy (Lageenergie):** Bodies that can do mechanical work due to their position.
        *   Examples: Pendulum at its highest point, stone in hand (can fall to Earth), roof tile.
    *   **Kinetic Energy (Bewegungsenergie):** Bodies in motion.
        *   Examples: Flying arrow, moving train, attacking bull.
    *   **Rotational Energy (Rotationsenergie):** Bodies that are rotating.
        *   Examples: Helicopter rotor, airplane propeller, turbine blade.
    *   **Elastic Energy (Elastische Energie):** Bodies that are stretched or compressed.
        *   Examples: Expander, trampoline, stretched bow.

*   **Acoustic Energy:** Contained in sound waves.
    *   Examples: Sounds, speech, siren.

*   **Electrical Energy:** A property of distributed electrical charges.
    *   Examples: Electricity from the socket, battery/accumulator, lightning.

*   **Magnetic Energy:** Generated by permanent magnets or current-carrying conductors.
    *   Examples: Horseshoe magnet, current-carrying coil, Earth's magnetic field.

*   **Radiation Energy (Strahlungsenergie):** The energy of electromagnetic radiation.
    *   Examples: Light, radio waves, microwaves, X-rays.

*   **Nuclear Energy (Kernenergie):** Released during radioactive decay, the fission of uranium or plutonium nuclei, and the fusion of certain hydrogen nuclei.
    *   Applications: Medicine, nuclear power plants, atomic and hydrogen bombs.

*   **Thermal Energy (Thermische Energie):** Stored in the movement of atoms or molecules of a substance.
    *   Examples: Solar energy, heat storage, radiator.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Infographic showing different energy forms with corresponding symbols and examples.]

#### Energy Transformation
Energy forms can be transformed. For example, potential energy can be converted into kinetic energy and rotational energy.
[INFO] Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another.

### Energie

#### Was ist Energie?
[DEFINITION] Energie ist die Fähigkeit, Arbeit zu verrichten.
[INFO] Der Physiker James Prescott Joule (1818-1889) untersuchte die Gleichwertigkeit von mechanischer Arbeit und Wärme.

**Einheit der Energie:** Joule (J)
`1 J = 1 Nm = 1 Ws`

Energie ist für verschiedene physikalische Vorgänge und alltägliche Aktivitäten unerlässlich, darunter:
*   Haushalt (Heizung, Beleuchtung, Geräte)
*   Betrieb (Produktion, Maschinen)

### Forces

#### What is a Physical Force?
In everyday life, the term "force" is used in various contexts (e.g., strength training, eyesight, persuasive power, washing power, power of love).
[DEFINITION] In physics, a force is a defined quantity that describes changes in motion, such as throwing a ball or braking a bicycle. It is a vector quantity and can be measured by various methods.

**Effects of a Force:**
When a force acts on a body, it can produce two effects:
1.  **Move the body:** A body moves when, for example, a football is kicked, a box is lifted, or a car is started.
2.  **Deform the body:** Deformation occurs primarily when the body cannot move. For example, if you sit on a ball, it is compressed.

[INFO] Forces are not visible; one only sees their effect on the body.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Illustrations showing a bowling ball being set in motion by force and a football being deformed upon impact.]

#### Force Multipliers (Kraftwandler)
Sometimes human muscle power is not enough to achieve a desired effect on a body. For this reason, tools and machines ("force multipliers") are used, e.g.:
*   **Pliers:** The lever principle is used to transmit force from the long handles to the short jaw head.
*   **Crane or excavator:** Construction machines amplify forces to lift heavy loads, dig, or transport.
    [INFO] In hydraulic systems, such as a hydraulic excavator, forces are transmitted or amplified with the help of fluids.
*   **Waste compactor:** Refuse vehicles are equipped with a press in the cargo area that compacts the waste.

**Practice Problem:**

#### Newton's Second Law (Fundamental Equation of Mechanics)
[DEFINITION] Forces cause changes in motion. The physical force acting on a body is closely related to the mass and acceleration of that body. Acceleration means that the body changes its speed. However, a body is also accelerated if its direction of motion changes. For example, if a body moves in a circle at a constant speed, it is accelerated because its direction changes.
*   The greater the force on a body, the greater its acceleration.
*   The greater the mass of the body, the more force is needed for the same acceleration.

[INFO] Sir Isaac Newton (1643-1727), the English natural scientist, formulated the laws of force in 1687 and became the namesake for the unit of force.

**Formula: Force, Mass, Acceleration:**
`F = m * a`
Where:
*   `F` is the symbol for force (English: force)
*   `m` is the mass of the body
*   `a` is the acceleration (English: acceleration)

[INFO] This equation is called the fundamental equation of mechanics.

**SI Unit of Force:** Newton (`[F] = N`)
`1 N = 1 kg * m/s²`
[DEFINITION] 1 Newton is the force required to impart an acceleration `a = 1 m/s²` to a body of mass `m = 1 kg`.

**Newton's Axioms (from handwritten notes):**
1.  **Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia):** A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.
2.  **Newton's Second Law (Fundamental Law of Dynamics):** The acceleration of a body is directly proportional to the force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. (This is `F = m * a` as described above).
3.  **Newton's Third Law (Law of Action-Reaction):** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

#### Effects of Physical Forces
The following diagram illustrates the effects of physical forces:
1.  A force can accelerate a body (positive acceleration `a`).
2.  A force can decelerate a body (negative acceleration `-a`).
3.  A force can change the direction of motion of a body.
4.  A force can deform a body.

#### Die Grundgleichung der Mechanik (Newtons zweites Gesetz)
[DEFINITION] Kräfte bewirken Bewegungsänderungen. Die physikalische Kraft, die auf einen Körper wirkt, steht in engem Zusammenhang mit der Masse und der Beschleunigung dieses Körpers. Beschleunigung bedeutet, dass der Körper seine Geschwindigkeit ändert. Ein Körper wird aber auch dann beschleunigt, wenn er seine Bewegungsrichtung ändert. Bewegt sich ein Körper beispielsweise mit gleichbleibender Geschwindigkeit im Kreis, so wird er beschleunigt, da er seine Richtung ändert.
*   Je größer die Kraft auf einen Körper ist, desto größer ist seine Beschleunigung.
*   Je größer die Masse des Körpers ist, desto mehr Kraft ist für die gleiche Beschleunigung nötig.

[INFO] Sir Isaac Newton (1643-1727), der englische Naturforscher, formulierte 1687 die Kraftgesetze und wurde Namensgeber für die Einheit der Kraft.

**Formel: Kraft, Masse, Beschleunigung:**
`F = m * a`
Dabei ist:
*   `F` das Formelzeichen für die Kraft (engl. force)
*   `m` die Masse des Körpers
*   `a` die Beschleunigung (engl. acceleration)

[INFO] Diese Gleichung wird als Grundgleichung der Mechanik bezeichnet.

**SI-Einheit der Kraft:** Newton (`[F] = N`)
`1 N = 1 kg * m/s²`
[DEFINITION] 1 Newton ist die Kraft, die erforderlich ist, einem Körper der Masse `m = 1 kg` die Beschleunigung `a = 1 m/s²` zu erteilen.

**Newtons Axiome (aus handschriftlichen Notizen):**
1.  **Newtons erstes Gesetz (Trägheitsgesetz):** Ein Körper bleibt in Ruhe oder in einer gleichförmigen Bewegung, solange keine äußere Kraft auf ihn einwirkt.
2.  **Newtons zweites Gesetz (Aktionsprinzip):** Wirkt auf einen Körper eine Kraft, wird er in Richtung der Kraft beschleunigt. Die Beschleunigung ist direkt proportional zur Kraft und indirekt proportional zur Masse des Körpers. (Dies ist `F = m * a` wie oben beschrieben).
3.  **Newtons drittes Gesetz (Wechselwirkungsprinzip):** Zu jeder Aktion gibt es eine gleich große, aber entgegengesetzte Reaktion.

Die folgende Abbildung zeigt die Wirkung physikalischer Kräfte:

2.  Eine Kraft kann einen Körper abbremsen (negative Beschleunigung `-a`).
3.  Eine Kraft kann die Bewegungsrichtung eines Körpers ändern.

### Gravitational Force and Buoyancy

#### Gravitational Force
[DEFINITION] Gravitational force is also called gravity or weight force. It acts on Earth as an attraction towards the Earth's center.

**Formula: Gravitational Force, Mass, Acceleration due to Gravity:**
`FG = m * g`
Where:
*   `FG` is the gravitational force (in N)
*   `m` is the mass (in kg)
*   `g` is the acceleration due to gravity (in m/s²)

**Acceleration due to Gravity on Earth's surface:** `g = 9.81 m/s² ≈ 10 m/s²`

[INFO] In everyday language, the terms mass and weight are often used interchangeably. In physics, however, a distinction should be made between these two concepts.
*   **Mass (`m`):** A fundamental quantity, its SI base unit is the kilogram (kg). It is a property of matter. This property remains constant even if the body's location changes (e.g., taking it to the moon).
*   **Weight Force (`FG`):** On the other hand, weight force depends on where the body is located. It is measured in Newtons (N). Thus, a body of mass `m` has a different weight force on Earth than on the Moon.

**Example:** Physically correct for the same person (on Earth with `g = 10 m/s²`):
*   "I weigh 600 Newtons" or
*   "I have a weight of 600 Newtons" or
*   "I have a mass of 60 kg."

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Earth with an object showing the gravitational force vector pointing towards the center.]

**Practice Problems:**

#### Buoyancy
The Earth's gravity pulls every mass towards the Earth's center. That's why water collects at the bottom as lakes and oceans. If you throw a stone into the water, it sinks. But why does a piece of wood float on the surface? There must be a force acting against gravity that causes buoyancy: the so-called buoyant force.

[DEFINITION] **Buoyancy:** The force opposite to gravity. The buoyant force acting on a body in a liquid or gas opposes the weight force of the body.

**It depends on:**
*   The density of the medium (liquid or gas) in which the body is located – the denser the medium, the greater the buoyancy.
*   The volume of the fluid (or gas) it displaces – the greater the displaced volume, the greater the buoyancy.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Illustration of an object in a fluid, showing the buoyant force acting upwards.]

**Archimedes' Principle:**
[DEFINITION] Archimedes' Principle states that the buoyant force is equal to the weight force of the displaced fluid or gas.

**Formula:**
`FA = ρ_medium * V_displaced * g = m_displaced * g`
Where:
*   `FA` is the buoyant force
*   `ρ_medium` is the density of the medium (liquid or gas)
*   `V_displaced` is the volume of the displaced fluid or gas
*   `g` is the acceleration due to gravity

[INFO] Archimedes (approx. 287-212 BC), a Greek scholar, discovered the Archimedes' Principle, named after him, in addition to the law of the lever.

**Example Calculation:**
A test tube is immersed in water such that 5 cm³ of water is displaced. Calculate the buoyant force on the test tube (`g = 9.81 m/s² ≈ 10 m/s²`, `ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³`).
**Solution:**
`FAuftrieb = V_displaced * ρ_fluid * g`
`FAuftrieb = 0.000005 m³ * 1000 kg/m³ * 10 m/s² = 0.05 N`

**Experiment: Measuring Buoyancy**
*   **Question:** What does the upward force acting on a body immersed in a liquid depend on?
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Place a glass with water on a scale and note the weight. The scale shows mass, but with the formula `FG = m * g` and `g ≈ 10 m/s²`, mass can easily be converted to weight.
    2.  Take a second, smaller container that fits into the water glass. The container should have a volume marking, e.g., a test tube with volume indications in 1-cm³ increments.
    3.  Now place the test tube in the water. The deeper it is immersed, the more force is needed – you have to push the test tube down more firmly. Note the immersion depth in 1-cm³ increments and the corresponding mass on the scale. Perform a second measurement to improve the accuracy of the results by averaging.
    4.  Repeat the experiment with a defined salt solution (e.g., 30 g salt per 100 ml).
*   **Interpretation:** The experiment shows: The greater the volume displaced by the test tube, the greater the buoyant force.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Experimental setup with a scale, water glass, and a test tube being immersed, showing force vectors (FG, FRA, FRG).]

**Influence of Density on Buoyancy (Hydrometer):**
Buoyancy also depends on the density of the liquid, as seen in the comparison of water and salt solution in the above experiment: The greater the density of the liquid, the greater the buoyant force.
A hydrometer, a device for measuring the density of liquids, shows the influence of density on buoyancy. Depending on the density, the hydrometer sinks deeper (e.g., in pure water) or less deep (e.g., in sugar or salt water). The deeper it sinks, the greater the volume of displaced liquid. If the weight force is equal to the buoyant force, the two forces cancel each other out, and the density can be read from the hydrometer's scale.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Hydrometer (Aräometer) in different liquids (e.g., water, salt water) showing varying immersion depths.]

#### Interaction of Gravitational Force and Buoyancy
The density difference determines whether an object sinks, floats, rises, or swims.

| Condition     | Relationship of Forces | Relationship of Densities | Example                           |
| :------------ | :--------------------- | :------------------------ | :-------------------------------- |
| **Sinking**   | `FA < FG`              | `ρ_fluid < ρ_body`        | Stones sink in water.             |
| **Hovering**  | `FA = FG`              | `ρ_fluid = ρ_body`        | A fish hovers in water at a certain depth. |
| **Rising**    | `FA > FG`              | `ρ_fluid > ρ_body`        | A submarine rises from the deep.  |
| **Swimming**  | `FA = FG`              | `ρ_fluid > ρ_body` (partially submerged) | A boat floats on water.           |

[INFO] Buoyancy also works in gases. With its help, for example, a hot air balloon can sail through the air because the hot air in the balloon has a lower density than the surrounding cold air.

#### Auftriebskraft
Die Schwerkraft der Erde zieht jede Masse Richtung Erdmittelpunkt. Deshalb sammelt sich auch das Wasser am Boden als See und Ozean. Wirft man einen Stein ins Wasser, so sinkt er zu Boden. Warum schwimmt aber ein Stück Holz an der Oberfläche? Es muss eine Kraft der Schwerkraft entgegenwirken, die für Auftrieb sorgt: die sogenannte Auftriebskraft.

[DEFINITION] **Auftriebskraft:** Die der Schwerkraft entgegengesetzte Kraft. Die Auftriebskraft eines Körpers in einer Flüssigkeit oder in einem Gas wirkt der Gewichtskraft des Körpers entgegen.

**Sie hängt ab:**
*   von der Dichte des Mediums (Flüssigkeit oder Gas), in dem sich der Körper befindet – je dichter das Medium, desto größer der Auftrieb;
*   vom Volumen der von ihm verdrängten Flüssigkeit (bzw. des von ihm verdrängten Gases) – je größer das verdrängte Volumen, desto größer der Auftrieb.

[DIAGRAM SUGGESTION: Illustration eines Objekts in einer Flüssigkeit, das die nach oben wirkende Auftriebskraft zeigt.]

**Archimedisches Prinzip:**
[DEFINITION] Das Archimedische Prinzip besagt, dass die Auftriebskraft gleich der Gewichtskraft der verdrängten Flüssigkeit bzw. des verdrängten Gases ist.

**Formel:**
`FA = ρ_medium * V_verdrängt * g = m_verdrängt * g`
Dabei ist:
*   `FA` die Auftriebskraft
*   `ρ_medium` die Dichte des Mediums (Flüssigkeit oder Gas)
*   `V_verdrängt` das Volumen der verdrängten Flüssigkeit bzw. des verdrängten Gases
*   `g` die Erdbeschleunigung

[INFO] Archimedes (ca. 287-212 v. Chr.), ein griechischer Gelehrter, entdeckte neben dem Hebelgesetz das nach ihm benannte Archimedische Prinzip.

**Lösung:**
`FAuftrieb = V_verdrängte Flüssigkeit * ρ_Flüssigkeit * g`
`FAuftrieb = 0,000005 m³ * 1000 kg/m³ * 10 m/s² = 0,05 N`

## [English]
### Fundamentals of Biology

#### What is Biology?
The word Biology is composed of two Greek words: `bíos` meaning "life" and `-logie` meaning "study," derived from `lógos` ("word").
[DEFINITION] Biology is therefore the branch (sub-discipline) of natural sciences that deals with living beings.

[DEFINITION] Living beings are called organisms in scientific language. In biology, for example, the structure of organisms, their development, and their relationships with their environment are studied.

[INFO] An authentic, living elephant has certain characteristics that distinguish it from a non-living object (e.g., a toy elephant). Appearance alone does not make something a living being.

#### Important Sub-disciplines of Biology
| Sub-discipline | Greek Root | Subject of Study          | Example                                       |
| :------------- | :--------- | :------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------- |
| **Zoology**    | `zóon` = animal | Animals                   | Scorpions belong to arachnids.                |
| **Botany**     | `botaniké` = plant | Plants                    | Mosses are simple plants.                     |
| **Microbiology** | `mikrós` = small | Microorganisms            | The bacterium *Vibrio cholerae* causes cholera. |
| **Ecology**    | `oikos` = household | Relationships of organisms to their environment | Bees pollinate plants.                        |

**Other related fields (from handwritten notes):**
*   **Anthropology:** Human biology.
*   **Genetics:** DNA, heredity.
*   **Paleontology:** Extinct organisms, fossils.

#### Nomenclature of Organisms
Organisms are usually given a two-part name in scientific literature. For example, modern humans are called *Homo sapiens*. The first part stands for the genus (Latin: *Homo* = human), the second part for the species (Latin: *sapiens* = wise). If the exact species is unknown, "species" (sp.) is written (e.g., *Vibrio sp.*).

[DEFINITION] **Binomial Nomenclature:** A two-part scientific name for an organism, consisting of the genus and species.

#### Methods of Biology
Biology uses many different methods to gain insights, including:
*   Description of an organism's appearance.
*   Observation of behavior.
*   Microscopic examinations.
*   Chemical examinations.

#### Characteristics of Life
Biologists have defined several differences between living beings and inanimate matter, the so-called characteristics of life.

Living beings distinguish themselves from inanimate material by various characteristics:
*   **Cellular structure:** All living beings are made up of one or more cells.
    *   **Unicellular organisms:** Consist of one cell (e.g., bacteria, paramecia, amoebae).
    *   **Multicellular organisms:** Consist of many cells (e.g., humans, animals, fungi, plants).
*   **Metabolism (Stoffwechsel):** Living beings take in substances from their environment, chemically convert them, and release waste products into the environment.
*   **Irritability/Responsiveness (Reizbarkeit):** Living beings perceive environmental stimuli and react to them (e.g., pupils constrict when looking into sunlight).
*   **Reproduction/Self-replication (Fortpflanzung):** All living beings produce offspring autonomously and transfer genetic material.
*   **Movement (Bewegung):** All living beings move. Some movements are only recognizable under a microscope (e.g., plants move towards light).
*   **Growth (Wachstum):** In multicellular organisms, growth occurs through cell division.

## [German]
### Grundlagen der Biologie

#### Was ist Biologie?
Das Wort Biologie ist aus zwei griechischen Wörtern zusammengesetzt: `bíos` bedeutet „Leben" und `-logie` „Lehre", abgeleitet von `lógos` („Wort").
[DEFINITION] Sie ist also jener Teilbereich (jene Teildisziplin) der Naturwissenschaften, der sich mit Lebewesen beschäftigt.

[DEFINITION] Lebewesen werden in der Fachsprache als Organismen bezeichnet. In der Biologie werden z. B. der Aufbau von Organismen, ihre Entwicklung, aber auch die Beziehungen der Lebewesen zu ihrer Umwelt erforscht.

[INFO] Ein echter, also lebender Elefant weist bestimmte Merkmale auf, die ihn von einem nicht lebendigen Objekt unterscheiden (z. B. einem Spielzeugelefanten). Das Aussehen allein macht kein Lebewesen aus.

#### Wichtige Teildisziplinen der Biologie
| Teildisziplin    | Griechische Wurzel | Untersuchungsgegenstand    | Beispiel                                      |
| :--------------- | :----------------- | :------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------- |
| **Zoologie**     | `zóon` = Tier      | Tiere                      | Skorpione gehören zu den Spinnentieren.       |
| **Botanik**      | `botaniké` = Pflanze | Pflanzen                   | Moose sind einfache Pflanzen.                 |
| **Mikrobiologie** | `mikrós` = klein   | Kleinstlebewesen           | Das Bakterium *Vibrio cholerae* verursacht Cholera. |
| **Ökologie**     | `oikos` = Haushalt | Beziehungen der Organismen zu ihrer Umwelt | Bienen bestäuben Pflanzen.                    |

**Weitere verwandte Gebiete (aus handschriftlichen Notizen):**
*   **Anthropologie:** Humanbiologie.
*   **Genetik:** DNA, Vererbung.
*   **Paläontologie:** Ausgestorbene Lebewesen, Fossilien.

#### Benennung von Organismen
Organismen werden in der wissenschaftlichen Literatur in der Regel mit einem zweiteiligen Namen angegeben. Der heutige Mensch heißt z. B. *Homo sapiens*. Der 1. Teil steht für die Gattung (lat. *Homo* = Mensch), der 2. Teil für die Art (lat. *sapiens* = weise). Ist die genaue Art nicht bekannt, wird *species* (sp.) geschrieben (z. B. *Vibrio sp.*).

[DEFINITION] **Binäre Nomenklatur:** Ein zweiteiliger wissenschaftlicher Name für einen Organismus, bestehend aus Gattungs- und Artnamen.

#### Methoden der Biologie
Die Biologie nutzt viele verschiedene Methoden, um zu Erkenntnissen zu gelangen, dazu gehören z. B.:
*   Beschreibung des Aussehens eines Organismus.
*   Beobachten des Verhaltens.
*   Mikroskopische Untersuchungen.
*   Chemische Untersuchungen.

#### Kennzeichen des Lebens
Biologinnen und Biologen haben einige Unterschiede zwischen Lebewesen und toter Materie definiert, die sogenannten Kennzeichen des Lebens.

Lebewesen unterscheiden sich von unbelebten Material durch verschiedene Lebenskennzeichen:
*   **Bestehen aus Zellen:** Alle Lebewesen sind aus einer oder mehreren Zellen aufgebaut.
    *   **Einzeller:** Bestehen aus 1 Zelle (z.B. Bakterien, Pantoffeltierchen, Amöben).
    *   **Vielzeller:** Bestehen aus vielen Zellen (z.B. Menschen, Tiere, Pilze, Pflanzen).
*   **Stoffwechsel:** Lebewesen nehmen feste, flüssige und gasförmige Stoffe auf, wandeln sie chemisch um und geben die Abfallstoffe an die Umwelt ab.
*   **Reizbarkeit:** Lebewesen nehmen Umweltreize wahr und reagieren darauf (z.B. die Pupillen verkleinern sich im Sonnenlicht).
*   **Eigenständige Vermehrung (Fortpflanzung):** Alle Lebewesen erzeugen eigenständig Nachkommen und übertragen Erbsubstanz.
*   **Bewegungsvorgänge:** Alle Lebewesen bewegen sich. Manche Bewegungen sind nur unter dem Mikroskop erkennbar (z.B. Pflanzen bewegen sich zum Licht).
*   **Wachstum:** Bei Vielzellern erfolgt das Wachstum durch Zellteilung.
