States of matter (Aggregatzustände)
Aggregatzustände
English
Definition
A state of matter (Aggregatzustand) describes the form and volume a substance has under given conditions. The state depends mainly on temperature and pressure; adding or removing heat can change it.
Note
Changes of state: solid → liquid is melting (Schmelzen); liquid → solid is freezing/solidifying (Erstarren); liquid → gas is vaporisation (Verdampfen); gas → liquid is condensation (Kondensieren); solid → gas is sublimation (Sublimieren); gas → solid is deposition (Resublimieren). Temperature and pressure affect transition temperatures.
Note
At normal pressure on Earth’s surface (~1 bar), helium does not solidify; liquid helium is an important coolant. Water at normal air pressure, p_0=1013 hPa, melts at 0°C and boils at 100°C. These thresholds apply at that pressure. In ice, water molecules form an ordered hexagonal structure; in water they are close but mobile, and in vapour they move freely and rapidly.
Exam Tip
Distinguish boiling from the general gas state: the state depends on both temperature and pressure. At exactly a phase-transition temperature, two phases may coexist; use the textbook’s stated conditions when classifying.

Worked exercises (book p. 99):

Ü 3.18 Water: at −30°C water is solid; at 20°C it is liquid; at 100°C it is at its normal-pressure boiling point (liquid and vapour can coexist during boiling); at 130°C it is gaseous, assuming normal pressure.

Ü 3.19 Hydrogen: melting point −259°C and boiling point −253°C. At 20°C it is gaseous. Yes, liquid hydrogen exists between its melting and boiling points, approximately −259°C to −253°C at the stated pressure; below the melting point it is solid.

Ü 3.20 Chip packet on a mountain: as altitude increases, external air pressure decreases. Gas trapped in the packet expands until pressures balance, so the flexible packet bulges. This is a pressure/volume effect, not the chips producing gas.

Ü 3.7 Particle dance (p. 93, related exercise): represent H₂O molecules with body = O and arms = H. Vapour: fast, free, chaotic movement; condensing: particles move closer and slow, with some intermolecular attractions; freezing: particles settle into an ordered hexagonal arrangement like a honeycomb.

Deutsch
Definition
Ein Aggregatzustand beschreibt, welche Form und welches Volumen ein Stoff unter bestimmten Bedingungen besitzt. Der Zustand hängt vor allem von Temperatur und Druck ab; durch Zufuhr oder Entzug von Wärme kann er verändert werden.
Note
Zustandsübergänge: fest → flüssig = Schmelzen; flüssig → fest = Erstarren; flüssig → gasförmig = Verdampfen; gasförmig → flüssig = Kondensieren; fest → gasförmig = Sublimieren; gasförmig → fest = Resublimieren. Temperatur und Druck beeinflussen die Übergangstemperaturen.
Note
Helium wird bei normalem Druck an der Erdoberfläche (~1 bar) als einziges Element nicht fest; flüssiges Helium ist ein wichtiges Kühlmittel. Wasser schmilzt bei normalem Luftdruck p_0=1013 hPa bei 0°C und siedet bei 100°C. Diese Werte gelten bei diesem Druck. Im Eis bilden Wassermoleküle eine geordnete hexagonale Struktur; im flüssigen Wasser sind sie nahe beieinander und beweglich, im Wasserdampf bewegen sie sich frei und schnell.
Exam Tip
Sieden ist nicht dasselbe wie der allgemeine gasförmige Zustand: Der Zustand hängt von Temperatur und Druck ab. Am Übergangspunkt können zwei Phasen gleichzeitig vorkommen; für Einordnungen gelten die im Buch genannten Bedingungen.

Density (Dichte)
Dichte
English
Definition
Mass density (Massendichte) is the ratio of a body’s mass m to its volume V: ρ=m/V. The SI unit is kg/m³; common alternatives include g/cm³ and kg/dm³. Density increases when mass increases at fixed volume or volume decreases at fixed mass.
Note
Density is distinct from population density (people per area) and particle density (particles per volume). Different materials can have different densities at the same volume; most materials become denser as they cool because their particles move closer. Exceptions exist, notably water near freezing. Measured examples: air at 20°C and sea level 0.0012 g/cm³=1.2 kg/m³; spruce 0.5 g/cm³=500 kg/m³; water 1.00 g/cm³=1000 kg/m³; platinum 21.45 g/cm³=21 450 kg/m³.
Exam Tip
Make units consistent before using ρ=m/V. Useful conversions: 1 g/cm³=1000 kg/m³ and 1 dm³=1 L. Volume of a rectangular solid is length × width × height. A scale measures mass; convert to weight force with F_G=mg only when force is needed.

Record repeated measurements and units; the source provides no numerical measurements, so no unique numerical answers exist.

Note
Conductivity (Leitfähigkeit), p. 94: a conductor transports energy or particles; electrical conductance G measures how well current flows, with unit Siemens, [G]=S. Metals conduct through mobile electrons; silver conducts best, while copper and aluminium are lighter/cheaper and widely used in cables. Insulators (e.g. rubber, glass, porcelain, diamond, distilled water, air) conduct very poorly. Semiconductor conductivity lies between these extremes; silicon and germanium conduct better as temperature releases charge carriers. Electrolytes conduct through ions. For Ü 3.11, insert each test object into a circuit gap: bright lamp = good conductor, dim = moderate conductor, dark = insulator. Classify the named objects experimentally; the source gives no observations, so individual test results are not specified.

Energy (Energie)
Energie
English
Definition
Energy (Energie) is the ability to do work. Its symbol is E, and its SI unit is the joule (J), the same unit as work W: 1 J=1 Nm=1 Ws. One joule is small: heating 1 kg of water by 1°C requires about 4190 J.
Note
Energy forms include mechanical (kinetic/motion and potential/position), acoustic, electrical, thermal, nuclear, radiant/light and magnetic energy; the class notes also list chemical energy. Energy can be transformed between forms; in an isolated system it is conserved, not created or destroyed. James Prescott Joule (1818–1889) studied the equivalence of mechanical work and heat, heating in current-carrying conductors and cooling of expanding gases.
Exam Tip
Identify the physical process, not the everyday setting, when naming an energy form. One situation can involve several forms and transformations.

Worked exercises (p. 99):

Ü 3.16 Properties of materials: examples beyond the listed properties: hardness (resistance to scratching/indentation), electrical conductivity (ability to carry current), solubility (ability to dissolve), viscosity (resistance to flow). Ü 3.17 Hardening: wet clay is hardened by drying and firing in a kiln, which removes water and changes the material irreversibly. Iron/steel can be hardened by heat treatment—heating and controlled rapid cooling (quenching); exact procedure depends on alloy.

Deutsch
Definition
Energie ist die Fähigkeit, Arbeit zu verrichten. Formelzeichen E, SI-Einheit Joule (J), dieselbe Einheit wie für Arbeit W: 1 J=1 Nm=1 Ws. Ein Joule ist klein: Um 1 kg Wasser um 1°C zu erwärmen, braucht man etwa 4190 J.

Energieformen: mechanische (Bewegungs- und Lageenergie), akustische, elektrische, thermische, Kern-, Strahlungs- und magnetische Energie; die Mitschrift nennt zusätzlich chemische Energie. Für die Zuordnung die physikalische Wirkung betrachten, nicht nur den Alltag. Ein Vorgang kann mehrere Energieformen und Umwandlungen enthalten.

Force and Newton’s axioms (Kraft und Newtonsche Axiome)
Kraft und Newtonsche Axiome
English
Definition
A force (Kraft) is a vector quantity recognised by its effects: it can change a body’s speed or direction (acceleration) or deform it. The mechanics equation is F=ma; force is measured in newtons: 1 N=1 kg · m/s². A body is matter with mass and occupies space.
Note
Greater force produces greater acceleration for fixed mass; greater mass needs more force for the same acceleration. Acceleration includes any change in velocity, including direction change in circular motion. Force arrows show direction and, to scale, magnitude. Tools and machines (force converters) transfer or amplify force, e.g. pliers (leverage), cranes/excavators and compactors. The book’s force effects are acceleration, slowing (negative acceleration), direction change and deformation.
Definition
Newton’s first law (inertia): a body remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless a net external force acts on it. Second law: net force causes acceleration in its direction, proportional to force and inversely proportional to mass; F=ma. Third law (action–reaction): interacting bodies exert equal and opposite forces on one another; they act on different bodies and therefore do not cancel each other on one body.
Exam Tip
Convert the requested quantity algebraically: F=ma, a=F/m, m=F/a. Check that kg and m/s² give N. A “heavier” body means larger mass in physics, not necessarily a larger force in every location.

Worked exercises (pp. 103–105; notes):

Ü 3.24 Car sensations: (a) accelerating: body tends to remain in its previous state; relative to car you feel pushed back. (b) braking: body tends to continue forward, so you lurch forward. (c) turning: inertia makes you feel pushed toward the outside; the car’s inward force changes direction.

Ü 3.26, m=10 kg: (a) a=0.1 m/s²; (b) 0.5 m/s²; (c) 1 m/s²; (d) 2 m/s².

Ü 3.27, a=1 m/s²: (a) 800 N; (b) 1200 N; (c) 1600 N.

Ü 3.28: (a) True: double mass needs double force for the same acceleration. (b) False: at equal force, the smaller mass accelerates more. (c) True under the same braking force and other conditions: half the mass gives twice the deceleration magnitude.

Ü 3.34: force is a vector, measured in N, and can accelerate, decelerate, change direction or deform a body; represent it by an arrow whose direction is the force direction and whose length indicates magnitude using a chosen scale.

Deutsch
Definition
Eine Kraft ist eine vektorielle Größe, erkennbar an ihrer Wirkung: Sie kann Geschwindigkeit oder Richtung ändern (beschleunigen) oder einen Körper verformen. Grundgleichung der Mechanik: F=ma; Einheit Newton: 1 N=1 kg · m/s². Ein Körper besteht aus Materie, hat Masse und nimmt Raum ein.
Note
Bei gleicher Masse bewirkt größere Kraft größere Beschleunigung; bei größerer Masse braucht man für gleiche Beschleunigung mehr Kraft. Beschleunigung bedeutet jede Änderung der Geschwindigkeit, auch eine Richtungsänderung bei Kreisbewegung. Kraftpfeile zeigen Richtung und maßstäblich die Größe. Werkzeuge und Maschinen (Kraftwandler) übertragen oder verstärken Kräfte: Zange (Hebel), Kran/Bagger, Müllpresse. Die Wirkungen im Buch sind Beschleunigen, Abbremsen (negative Beschleunigung), Richtungsänderung und Verformen.
Definition
1. Newtonsches Axiom (Trägheit): Ein Körper bleibt in Ruhe oder gleichförmiger geradliniger Bewegung, solange keine resultierende äußere Kraft auf ihn wirkt. 2. Axiom: Eine resultierende Kraft beschleunigt den Körper in Kraftrichtung; die Beschleunigung ist proportional zur Kraft und umgekehrt proportional zur Masse: F=ma. 3. Axiom (Wechselwirkung): Zu jeder Aktion gibt es eine gleich große, entgegengesetzt gerichtete Reaktion. Die Kräfte wirken auf verschiedene Körper und heben einander daher nicht an einem einzelnen Körper auf.
Exam Tip
Gesuchte Größe umstellen: F=ma, a=F/m, m=F/a. Mit kg und m/s² erhält man N. „Schwerer“ bedeutet in der Physik größere Masse; die Gewichtskraft kann sich je nach Ort ändern.

5
Gravitation and buoyancy (Gravitationskraft und Auftriebskraft)
Gravitationskraft und Auftriebskraft
English
Definition
Gravitational force / weight force (Gravitationskraft, Schwerkraft, Gewichtskraft) is attraction between masses. Earth’s gravity pulls bodies toward Earth’s centre. Near Earth’s surface, F_G=mg, with g=9.81 m/s²≈10 m/s²; F_G is in N and m in kg. Mass is an intrinsic property measured in kg; weight force depends on local gravitational acceleration and is measured in N.
Definition
Buoyant force (Auftriebskraft) acts opposite to gravity on a body in a liquid or gas. Archimedes’ principle states that it equals the weight of the displaced medium: F_A=ρ_{medium}V_{displaced}g=m_{displaced}g. Greater displaced volume or greater medium density gives greater buoyancy.
Note
Floating/sinking compares F_A with F_G and body/medium densities: sink if F_A<F_G (body denser); neutral suspension if equal densities and forces; rise if F_A>F_G; a floating body at the surface has equilibrium forces and displaces enough fluid to balance its weight. A hot-air balloon rises because hot air inside is less dense than surrounding air. A submarine changes buoyancy by filling ballast spaces with water or air. A hydrometer floats deeper in lower-density liquid and its scale indicates density; at equilibrium F_A=F_G.
Exam Tip
Convert volume to m³ before using SI densities: 1 m³=1000 dm³=1 000 000 cm³=1 000 000 000 mm³; 1 dm³=1 L; 1 L=1000 ml. Use g≈10 when the exercise does. For a floating body, set F_A=F_G.

Biology: subdisciplines, nomenclature and characteristics of life (Biologie: Teilgebiete, Nomenklatur, Kennzeichen des Lebens)
Biologie: Teilgebiete, Nomenklatur, Kennzeichen des Lebens
English
Definition
Biology (Biologie) is the natural-science discipline that studies living things. The word combines Greek bíos (“life”) and -logie (“study/teaching”). Living things are called organisms (Organismen); biology studies their structure, development and relationships with their environment.
Note
Important subdisciplines include zoology (Zoologie)—animals; botany (Botanik)—plants; microbiology (Mikrobiologie)—microorganisms; and ecology (Ökologie)—relationships among organisms and their environment. Class notes additionally name anthropology/human biology, genetics/heredity and DNA, and palaeontology/fossils and extinct organisms. Methods include describing appearance, observing behaviour, microscopy and chemical analysis.
Definition
Binomial nomenclature (binäre Nomenklatur) gives an organism a two-part scientific name: the first word is the genus (Gattung) and the second the species epithet (Art). Example: Homo sapiens—Homo is the genus (“human”), sapiens the species epithet (“wise”); Vibrio cholerae has genus Vibrio and epithet cholerae. If the species is unknown, use sp. after the genus, e.g. Vibrio sp. Scientific names are written in italics (or underlined when handwritten), with genus capitalised and epithet lower-case.
Definition
Characteristics of life (Kennzeichen des Lebens) distinguish organisms from inanimate matter: cellular organisation, metabolism, responsiveness to stimuli, independent reproduction and movement. Notes also identify growth as a characteristic.
Exam Tip
Movement alone does not prove something is alive: a toy can move mechanically. Look for the full set of life characteristics. A real elephant and toy elephant may look similar, but only the organism has cells, metabolism, growth, stimulus responses and reproduction.

Worked exercise Ü 4.1 (p. 134): propose distinguishing features for living and non-living examples, then compare with the criteria on p. 135. A real elephant is cellular, metabolises food/water and oxygen, grows, responds to stimuli, reproduces and moves; a toy elephant does none of these biologically, even if pushed. No single visible trait such as movement or appearance is sufficient by itself.
